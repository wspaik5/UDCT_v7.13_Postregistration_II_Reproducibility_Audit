# UDCT v7.13 Post-registration II
## Hydrus I Jeans Sign-Boundary and Hessian Reproducibility Audit

**Author:** Won Shik Paik — Independent Researcher, Auckland, New Zealand  
**Publication date:** 22 October 2026  
**Zenodo DOI:** https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23559995  
**GitHub:** https://github.com/wspaik5/UDCT_v7.13_Postregistration_II_Reproducibility_Audit

## Abstract

This follow-up technical report audits the reproducibility and evidentiary limits of numerical records from UDCT v7.13 Post-registration I. It examines Jeans sign-boundary diagnostics and Hessian-related calculations for observationally informed Hydrus I dwarf-galaxy models.

The original archive contains 24 candidate sign-transition brackets, 48 endpoint evaluations, and 288 saved six-arm numerical profiles. Under the registered strict classification criteria, 18 endpoint evaluations are robust-positive, 30 remain unresolved, and none supplies a robust-negative common-domain witness. Consequently, none of the 24 candidate brackets is confirmed as a strict sign transition.

The audit distinguishes native-grid minima from minima calculated on a shared radial comparison domain, and separates archived numerical evidence from independent physical validation.

## Key findings

- 24 candidate sign-transition brackets examined.
- 48 endpoint evaluations and 288 saved numerical profiles documented.
- 18 robust-positive, 30 unresolved, and 0 robust-negative endpoint evaluations under the strict common-domain criteria.
- 0 of 24 candidate brackets validated as strict sign transitions.
- Native-grid negative values cannot automatically be interpreted as common-domain negative witnesses.

## Reproducibility scope and limitations

This release documents retrospective checks of archived numerical arrays, source-code provenance, and selected validation controls. These checks do not constitute a complete independent rerun of all original physical calculations.

The report does not establish a self-consistent three-dimensional modified-gravity Hessian solution or a direct likelihood fit to individual Hydrus I stellar line-of-sight velocity observations. Observationally informed model inputs should not be confused with direct star-by-star observational validation.

The findings are an audit of the archived evidence, not a claim that MOND or any modified-gravity theory has been conclusively confirmed or falsified.
